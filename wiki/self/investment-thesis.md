# Investment Thesis

Yago's explicit framework for how the [[Finances|portfolio]] is constructed. Two distinct theses, applied to two distinct buckets.

## 1. Dividend aligned with growth

**Philosophy**: Luiz Barsi / [AGF (Ações Garantem o Futuro)](https://acoesgarantem.com.br/) methodology. Focus on **consistent monthly dividend income** from stable, mature public companies with modest but durable growth.

**Holdings**: 100% of the public stock portfolio (R$ 137k).

**Why the concentration is intentional, not accidental**: the [[Finances]] risk assessment flags "Portfolio concentration (4 names, 1 country, financial-sector heavy)" as Medium severity. Per [[Source: Investment Thesis]], this concentration is a deliberate methodology choice — Barsi-style focuses on a small number of dividend-paying blue chips. The risk is real, but Yago has selected it knowingly.

**Tension to surface**: while the concentration is intentional, it does **not** address the FX hedge gap flagged High in [[Finances]]. Barsi methodology is BRL-denominated by design — it doesn't conflict with USD diversification, but it also doesn't deliver it. The [[International Relocation]] thesis still requires a separate USD allocation.

## 2. Growth

**Philosophy**: invest in companies where Yago has **deep insider knowledge** of operations and current state, and which have **strong growth potential**. The information advantage justifies bypassing public-market diversification.

**Holdings**: [[Fluencypass]] equity only — R$ 75k cumulative buy-in (current value R$ 72.5k).

This is the **entire growth bucket** of the portfolio. No other company qualifies under this thesis right now.

## How the two theses interact

Per [[Source: Investment Thesis]] (2026-05-04), the dividend bucket is for monthly income; the growth bucket is for asymmetric upside via insider information.

**Implications**:

- The R$ 2,500/mo equity buy-in at [[Fluencypass]] is the **only growth position being added monthly**. Pausing the buy-in (per the open decision in [[Financial Goals]]) effectively pauses the entire growth thesis.
- Any "alternative growth bet" Yago might consider (e.g., another startup, tech ETF) would either: (a) need to qualify as "deep insider knowledge" under his own framing, or (b) require him to expand the thesis.
- Public tech / growth ETFs (NASDAQ, IVVB11, etc.) **don't fit** either bucket as currently defined — they're neither high-yield dividend payers nor insider-knowledge growth bets. If FX hedge is added via S&P 500 / NASDAQ ETFs, that opens a **third bucket** the thesis doesn't yet name.

## Growth-trajectory analysis (added 2026-05-04 13:31)

The 13% YoY MRR growth is the single most important new data point for evaluating the growth thesis. Three implications:

### 1. Pure-organic 5-10x is off the table at this growth rate

A SaaS exit at 5-10x of buy-in price typically requires either:
- 40-80% YoY growth sustained for 3-5 years (organic story), or
- A combination of solid growth + meaningful multiple expansion (premium / strategic / AI category leader)

At 13% YoY:
- 3-year revenue multiplier: 1.44x
- 5-year revenue multiplier: 1.84x

Even with revenue 1.84x in 5 years, the stake math requires **multiple expansion of ~2.7x for 5x exit** or **~5.4x for 10x exit**. Not impossible, but a much narrower path than "the company grows into the valuation."

### 2. The B2B pivot is the only credible accelerator

If FP hits the 2026 plan (B2B 8% → 30% of revenue, requiring B2B revenue ~4x while rest holds flat), blended growth could move toward 30-40% YoY. At 35% YoY sustained for 5 years, revenue compounds 4.5x — which **does** support 5x on the stake at flat multiple, and 10x with modest premium.

But that scenario requires:
- B2B target hit by end of 2026 (currently 8%, plan = 30%)
- Sustained acceleration beyond 2026, not just one good year
- Pricing power and retention in B2B (FP doesn't yet have proven track record there)

### 3. The growth thesis is now a "bet on the B2B pivot landing", not "bet on FP being a great company"

This is a sharper, more falsifiable framing. It points to specific things to watch:
- Q2 2026: B2B revenue share moving above 12-15%?
- Q3 2026: B2B revenue share above 18-22%?
- Q4 2026: at or near 30%?

If B2B share doesn't visibly accelerate quarterly through 2026, the multiple-expansion scenario becomes the only remaining path, which is a much weaker base for keeping R$ 2,500/mo invested in the buy-in.
