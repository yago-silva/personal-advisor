# Source: Investment Thesis

Yago's explicit framework for portfolio construction. Two theses:

1. **Dividend aligned with growth** — slow, stable, public-company dividend payers. Inspired by Luiz Barsi / AGF methodology. Holdings: BBAS3, BBSE3, ISAE4, CXSE3.
2. **Growth** — companies with deep insider knowledge and high growth potential. Holdings: only [[Fluencypass]].

## Key takeaways

- The R$ 137k stock portfolio is intentionally Barsi-style — focused on consistent monthly dividend income from large-cap financial-sector stocks. The 100% BRL / 72% financial concentration ([[Finances]] risk row) is **not accidental** — it's a methodology choice.
- [[Fluencypass]] equity is the **only** "growth" position in the portfolio. The R$ 75k buy-in is the entire growth bucket.
- Yago has insider visibility on Fluencypass that he does not have on any public company — by his own framing this is the justification for it being the growth bet.

## Notable observations (advisor flags, not in source)

- **B2C churn 9-11%/month is high**. At 10% monthly compounded, annual retention ≈ 28% — most customers gone within 12 months. LTV of 8-12 months is consistent with this. This is the structural reason FP wants B2B to dominate (B2B churn typically 1-3% / month).
- **Revenue per employee ≈ R$ 125k/year** — low for SaaS (US benchmark $200k+), suggests high-touch services component.
- **B2B is 6 months old (Nov 2025 launch)** with 8% of revenue. Going to 30% in 2026 means B2B revenue must roughly **4x** in 12 months while keeping the rest stable. Aggressive but not impossible.
- "The company does not have a clear vision about other markers, for example: churn rate" — Yago's own language. Suggests metric instrumentation gap inside FP. Worth probing if it limits valuation in any future M&A diligence.
